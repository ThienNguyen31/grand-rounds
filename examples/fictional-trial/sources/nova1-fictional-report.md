## Methods (p. 2)

NOVA-1 was a phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in adults with completely
resected stage IIB to IV cutaneous melanoma. Patients were randomly assigned in a 2:1
ratio, stratified by stage (IIB to IIIB vs IIIC to IV), to receive a personalised
neoantigen vaccine (1 mg intramuscularly every 3 weeks for 9 doses) or placebo; all
patients received anti-PD-1 therapy (400 mg intravenously every 6 weeks for 9 cycles). The
primary end point was recurrence-free survival.

## Results (p. 4)

Of 1204 patients who underwent screening, 342 were not randomised (insufficient tumour
tissue, 201; not disease-free, 88; consent withdrawn, 53). A total of 862 patients were
randomised: 575 to vaccine plus anti-PD-1 and 287 to placebo plus anti-PD-1.

Recurrence-free survival at 24 months was 79.1% with vaccine plus anti-PD-1 and 67.0% with
placebo plus anti-PD-1 (hazard ratio for recurrence or death, 0.62; 95% CI, 0.47 to 0.81).
Recurrence or death occurred in 128 and 98 patients, respectively (Fig. 2).

Overall survival data were immature (hazard ratio for death, 0.81; 95% CI, 0.52 to 1.27). Death occurred in 69 and 39 patients, respectively; overall survival at 24 months was 89.1% and 86.7% (Fig. 3).

## Safety (p. 6, Table 3)

Adverse events of grade 3 or higher occurred in 21.4% of patients in the vaccine group and
19.5% in the placebo group. Injection-site pain occurred in 48.9% vs 9.1%, fever in 30.1%
vs 6.3%, fatigue in 41.2% vs 33.8%, diarrhoea in 20.2% vs 18.8%, and pruritus in 18.4% vs
16.0%.
